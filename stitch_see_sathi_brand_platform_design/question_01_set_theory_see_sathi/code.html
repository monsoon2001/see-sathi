Compulsory Mathematics Chapter 1: Sets & Cardinality
Question No. 11 (Group C) Weightage: 4 Marks CDC 2080 Board Exam
psychology PROBLEM STATEMENT

In a survey of 100 students of a secondary school in Kathmandu, 65 like tea, 45 like coffee, and 15 like neither of them. By drawing a Venn diagram, calculate the number of students who like both tea and coffee, and find the number of students who like tea only.

1

Representation of Cardinality Data

Defining universal set and subset symbols

1.0 Mark

Let T and C represent the set of students who like tea and coffee respectively.

n(U) = 100
n(T) = 65
n(C) = 45
n(T ∪ C)' = 15
2

Formula Application for Union

Computing cardinality of combined preference

1.0 Mark

By standard set theory cardinality formula for complement of union:

n(T ∪ C) = n(U) - n(T ∪ C)'

Substituting the given parameters:

n(T ∪ C) = 100 - 15 = 85

Thus, 85 students like at least one beverage (tea or coffee).

3

Finding Intersection n(T ∩ C)

Calculating students who enjoy both beverages

1.0 Mark

Using the principle of inclusion and exclusion for two finite sets:

n(T ∪ C) = n(T) + n(C) - n(T ∩ C)

Rearranging the equation to solve for the unknown intersection:

n(T ∩ C) = n(T) + n(C) - n(T ∪ C)
n(T ∩ C) = 65 + 45 - 85 = 110 - 85 = 25

check_circle Result (i): Exactly 25 students like both tea and coffee.

4

Venn Diagram Illustration & Tea Only Calculation

Diagrammatic modeling and disjoint cardinality

1.0 Mark

Number of students who like tea only is denoted by no(T):

no(T) = n(T) - n(T ∩ C)
no(T) = 65 - 25 = 40

Additionally, Coffee only: no(C) = n(C) - n(T ∩ C) = 45 - 25 = 20.

check
Final Answer Summary

(i) Number of students who like both tea and coffee: n(T ∩ C) = 25 students

(ii) Number of students who like tea only: no(T) = 40 students

Calculations validated against: 40 (tea only) + 20 (coffee only) + 25 (both) + 15 (neither) = 100 (Total Universe).
